<summary>Project Overview & AI Prototype</summary>

This is an AI‑powered chatbot application hosted on XJTLU internal AI‑Agent platform. The target users are Year 1 XJTLU students who will complete major selection at the end of Year 1.

Students can ask natural‑language questions about XJTLU major requirements, GPA thresholds, course workload and career prospects. The AI assistant provides tailored information to support their major decision‑making.

Note: The AI chatbot link below is XJTLU internal resource. XJTLU campus network or XJTLU VPN is required to access this agent.

<details>
<summary>Research Questions</summary>

1. Can this AI chatbot help Year 1 XJTLU students retrieve accurate and clear information for major selection?

2. Can users efficiently get answers to their major‑related questions by interacting with this AI assistant?

3. Do Year 1 students trust the AI‑generated answers and consider this tool usable for their major choice?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Research Rationale</summary>

Year 1 XJTLU students face major selection, but information about majors is scattered across official websites, social media and senior students’ experience.

Students spend much time collecting and comparing major information manually.

This study uses a comparative perspective: we compare the user experience of getting major information via our AI assistant against the traditional way (searching official websites and forums).

We aim to explore whether an AI chatbot can lower the information‑search burden for students.

</details>

<summary>Experiment Plan</summary>

3.1 Evaluation Methods
We combine usability testing, post‑test survey, and semi‑structured interview.

Usability testing: Participants complete 3 predefined tasks on our AI agent. We record task performance.

Post‑test survey: Collect quantitative usability and trust scores after tasks.

Semi‑structured interview: Gather qualitative feedback about pain points and suggestions.


3.2 Sampling Method
Convenience sampling. Recruit 5–8 Year 1 XJTLU students preparing for major selection.

Inclusion criteria: Current Year 1 students, have not confirmed their final major.


3.3 Data to Collect

Quantitative Data
1. Task completion time for each task

2. Task success rate (whether participants finish tasks correctly)

3. SUS (System Usability Scale) score

4. 5‑point Likert score for trust in AI responses


Qualitative Data
1. Spoken comments during usability test

2. Answers in post-test interview

3. Records of AI errors, missing or confusing outputs


3.4 Predefined User Tasks
Participants will complete these tasks on the XJTLU AI‑Agent chatbot:

1. Task 1: Ask the AI about GPA requirements and prerequisite courses for the HCI major at XJTLU.

2. Task 2: Your GPA is 70, ask the AI to recommend suitable majors.

3. Task 3: Ask the AI to compare the workload and career prospects of Computer Science and HCI.

<summary>Reflection on Study Implementation</summary>

1. Limitations found during pilot data collection

The AI may occasionally generate incorrect or outdated XJTLU major information, which reduces user trust.

Some participants do not know how to phrase their questions well, so they struggle to get ideal replies.

Small sample size (only 5 participants), results cannot be generalised to all Year1 students.

This AI agent is an internal XJTLU resource; users outside campus without VPN cannot access it.


2. Improvement suggestions

Add example prompt buttons on the chat page to guide users to write better questions.

Build a fact-check knowledge base to reduce AI hallucinations about XJTLU major rules.

Add a clear disclaimer on the webpage to remind users that AI answers are not official information.


3. Study reflection

During usability testing, we learned that user trust is critical for students to accept AI tools for high-stakes decisions like major selection.

Usability is not only about interface operation, but also the accuracy of the AI content.
